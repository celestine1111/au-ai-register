Australian Public AI Register

Open data on how Australian governments spend on artificial intelligence, who they buy it from, who they hire, and how the migration intake is composed. Every row carries the URL it came from and the date it was retrieved.

Why this exists

Australia publishes a great deal of relevant data and joins almost none of it up. Contract notices sit on AusTender, migration composition sits in ABS SDMX endpoints, provider sanctions sit with ASQA, and parliamentary quotes sit in Hansard XML. Answering a question that spans two of them currently takes a week, so mostly nobody does.

Provenance

Three columns appear in every dataset and are enforced by continuous integration:

ColumnMeaning
source_urlThe exact URL the row came from. For API-derived rows this is the precise query, so it can be re-run to reproduce the figure.
source_retrieved_atISO 8601 retrieval timestamp.
source_tierprimary for a public record, claim for a company announcement that is public but promotional.

The build fails on a blank provenance column, an invalid tier, or a source_url that is a bare homepage. A front page changes daily and cannot evidence a historical figure.

Verify any figure yourself

curl -H "Accept: application/vnd.sdmx.data+csv;labels=both" "<source_url from the row>"

Every ingest is re-runnable and idempotent. If a figure cannot be reproduced from its source_url, that is a defect: open an issue.

What this measures, and what it does not

Net overseas migration is not visa grants. NOM uses the ABS 12-in-16-month residency rule. Home Affairs visa grant counts measure something different, and the two legitimately differ. They are not interchangeable.

"Temporary visa — Total" includes visitors, who are not a migration programme intake in the sense most policy discussion means. Use the component rows for questions about programme settings.

Visa group is not occupation. This dataset supports statements about the composition of the intake by visa class, not about which occupations migrants enter.

Who publishes this

Compiled and published by mindiam, which also runs AI Policy Watch, a clearly labelled opinion section arguing a position on Australian AI and migration policy.

We say so because you should know it. The data here is not selected, filtered or framed to support that position. The methodology is published in full, the sources and their known problems are documented, and the git history is a permanent audit trail of every change to every number.
